The $15 Fitness App Migration Checklist: Format Routines and Structure Pricing Tiers

Moving your fitness coaching business from a generic social media group or clunky spreadsheet system into a dedicated mobile app requires more than just picking a template. If you migrate your workout routines and pricing tiers without a strict structural plan, you will lose clients. A botched migration causes broken links, missing workout history, and billing confusion. Based on standard application and website migration protocols, you need a specialized checklist to preserve your content and revenue during this transition [1][2][3].

Here is the concrete, step-by-step checklist to format your routines and structure your pricing tiers for a mobile app launch.

Phase 1: Pre-Migration Content Audit

Before you build a single routine in your new app, you must define your migration goals and assess your existing content [1]. If you migrate garbage, you get a garbage app.

  1. Inventory your current routines: List every workout program you currently use. Note the format (video, text, PDF) and the equipment required.
  2. Identify high-value content: Determine which programs your clients actually use and log consistently. Discard or archive outdated routines that cause confusion.
  3. Back up everything: Export all your existing client data, workout histories, and media files. If you switch platforms and an unforeseen error causes content to disappear, you need a local backup to restore your library [3].
  4. Set measurable success metrics: Define what a successful migration looks like using SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound) targets [1]. For example: "Retain 90% of current monthly subscribers through the migration window" or "Reduce client support tickets regarding workout access by 50%."

Phase 2: Format Routines for Mobile

Mobile screens demand strict formatting. A routine that works on a printed PDF fails on a phone if the text is too dense or the videos are too large.

  1. Standardize naming conventions: Use a clear structure for every routine. Example: [Day] - [Focus] - [Duration]. A routine named "Monday - Lower Body - 45m" is instantly scannable.
  2. Break down instructions into micro-steps: Do not write paragraphs. Use strict sets, reps, and rest times. Format every exercise block exactly the same way: - Exercise Name - Sets x Reps - Rest Time - Execution Notes (1-2 short sentences)
  3. Optimize media: Compress all demonstration videos. Large videos cause app lag and consume client data plans. Standardize your video format across the entire library before uploading.
  4. Map URLs correctly: If your new app changes the structure of how clients access routines, ensure old links map to the new content [2]. Your canonicals and redirects should all be listed so they don’t end up pointing to the wrong pages during migration [2].

Phase 3: Structure Pricing Tiers

A new app gives you a clean slate to fix your pricing. If you change prices, you must communicate this across all levels of your organization to manage expectations [1].

  1. Define your tiers: Keep it simple. Three tiers work best for fitness coaching: - Tier 1 (Self-Guided): App access only. Pre-built routines, no custom adjustments. - Tier 2 (Hybrid): App access plus weekly form-check videos or group Q&A calls. - Tier 3 (1:1 Coaching): Fully custom routines inside the app, direct messaging, and weekly check-ins.
  2. Migrate existing billing: Map your current clients to the closest new tier. If a client pays $100/month now and your new Tier 2 costs $120, decide if you will honor the old rate or require an upgrade. State this explicitly in your migration welcome email.
  3. Preserve content quality for paid tiers: Ensure that high-value content is accurately moved and remains accessible only to the correct paying tier [2]. Do not accidentally lock premium routines behind your free or lowest tier.
  4. Expect a temporary dip: Plan for a temporary drop in revenue and user engagement during the transition [3]. Schedule the migration after a busy time in your business, not during a peak challenge launch [3].

Phase 4: The Migration and Post-Launch

  1. Test in a development space: Set up your new app structure in a closed environment. Do not invite clients until you have run through the SEO testing and user flow yourself [3].
  2. Update internal links: Ensure all internal links point to the correct pages post-migration [2]. If a routine references a mobility video, the link must direct to the new app location, not an old YouTube URL.
  3. Execute the switch: Send a clear, three-part email sequence to your clients. Tell them what is happening, when it is happening, and exactly how to log in to the new app.
  4. Monitor and optimize: Go live and watch the metrics you set in Phase 1. If client success drops, address the specific broken routine or billing error immediately [1].
