The Local SEO Weekend Kit: A One-Location Operator's 90-Day Plan

Most local SEO advice is written for agencies running 50-location rollups. If you run a single-location service business — a plumber in Toledo, a dentist in Tacoma, a divorce attorney in Tallahassee — you don't need a 200-location playbook. You need to do four things well, in order, and you can do them in a weekend, then maintain them over 90 days.

This is that plan.

What "done" looks like at the end of 90 days

Before you touch anything, write down your starting point. Pull these four numbers today:

  1. Your Google Business Profile views (last 90 days) — found in GBP Insights under "Performance."
  2. Your current star rating and total review count.
  3. The number of citations (mentions of your Name, Address, Phone) you can find by searching your business name + city and your phone number.
  4. Your ranking position for your single most valuable keyword — e.g., "emergency plumber Toledo" — checked from an incognito window on a phone, not your office Wi-Fi.

Write them on a sticky note. You'll compare against them in 90 days.

Weekend Day 1: Google Business Profile (the only profile that matters)

For a single-location service business, GBP drives more calls than every other channel combined. BrightLocal's local SEO industry surveys have repeatedly found GBP to be the single most influential factor in local pack rankings [1]. Get this right first.

The 90-minute GBP pass:

  • Primary category. Pick the one service you do most and make the most money from. Not "plumber" if you're really a "drain cleaning service." The category must match what a customer would type.
  • Secondary categories. Add up to 9 more, but only ones you actually perform. Google's own guidelines explicitly prohibit adding categories you don't genuinely offer [2].
  • Business description. 750 characters max. First sentence: what you do and where. Second sentence: who you serve. Third sentence: what makes you different (years in business, specialty, warranty). No keyword stuffing — Google's spam policy explicitly targets that [3].
  • Hours. Special hours for holidays. Set them now, before November.
  • Phone. Use a local number, not an 800. Call tracking numbers are allowed but must be set as a secondary number, with your real NAP as primary — Google's guidelines are explicit on this [4].
  • Photos. Upload 10 real photos today: exterior (different angles, different seasons if you have them), interior, your team working, your vehicles, a finished job. GBP listings with photos receive a meaningfully higher share of clicks than those without, per Google's own case studies [5]. Don't use stock photos. Don't use AI-generated photos. Customers can tell.
  • Services. List every service with a one-line description and a price range if you publish one anywhere. If you don't publish prices, leave the price field blank — don't invent them.
  • Posts. Add one Google Post today. Anything. A recent job, a tip, a seasonal reminder. Posts expire after 6 months, so this is a recurring task, not a one-time fix.

Verification status check. If your profile is "suspended," "disabled," or "needs verification," stop everything else and fix that first. Nothing else matters until GBP is live.

Weekend Day 2: Citations, Reviews, and On-Page

Citations (60 minutes)

A citation is any online mention of your business Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP). Consistency matters more than volume. If your NAP says "Suite 4" on one site and "Ste. 4" on another, that's a soft inconsistency Google has to resolve.

The minimum viable citation set for a US single-location business:

  • Apple Maps (via Apple Business Connect)
  • Bing Places
  • Facebook Business Page
  • Yelp
  • Better Business Bureau, if you're accredited
  • Your top 3 industry directories (e.g., for a plumber: Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack)
  • Yellow Pages / Superpages
  • Nextdoor Business Page
  • Chamber of Commerce for your city

Use a tool like Whitespark, BrightLocal, or Moz Local to find the ones you already have and the ones you're missing. If you don't have a budget for a tool, search your phone number in quotes — every site that returns is a citation you have.

NAP audit rule: Pick one canonical format. For example:

Toledo Drain Pros 1234 Monroe St, Suite 4 Toledo, OH 43604 (419) 555-0142

Use that exact string everywhere. Update the ones that don't match. Don't create new citations until the existing ones are consistent.

Reviews (30 minutes of setup, then ongoing)

You can't buy reviews. You can't trade reviews. Google's review spam policy is explicit: no fake reviews, no incentivized reviews, no reviews from employees [6]. What you can do is ask.

The ask script that works:

"Hey [name], glad we could help with [specific job]. If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps other folks in [city] find us. Here's the direct link: [short link to your GBP review form]."

Text it the same day you finish the job. Not two weeks later. The conversion rate from same-day text asks is dramatically higher than email follow-ups — this is one of the more consistently reported findings in BrightLocal's annual local business review surveys [7].

Respond to every review, positive or negative, within 48 hours. Keep responses under 80 words. Don't argue. Don't include keywords. Don't mention competitors.

On-Page (90 minutes)

Your website needs exactly three things for local SEO:

  1. A page titled for your primary service and city. Example: Drain Cleaning in Toledo, OH | Toledo Drain Pros. Not "Home," not "Welcome," not "Our Services."
  2. Your NAP in the footer of every page, matching the canonical format above exactly.
  3. An embedded Google Map on your contact page showing your location.

That's the floor. Anything beyond that (schema markup, location pages, service area pages) is incremental and only matters once the floor is solid.

The 90-Day Maintenance Calendar

The weekend gets you to "done once." The 90 days keep you there.

Weekly (15 minutes): - Check GBP Insights. Note views, calls, direction requests, and website clicks. - Respond to any new reviews. - Post one Google Post.

Monthly (60 minutes): - Run a citation check. Fix any new inconsistencies. - Check your top 3 competitors' GBP photos and posts. Note what they're doing that you're not. - Review your top 10 GBP search queries. These are the actual phrases people typed to find you. Make sure your business description and website copy reflect them.

Quarterly (3 hours): - Re-rank your primary keyword from an incognito mobile browser. - Audit your top 10 citations for NAP consistency. - Refresh your GBP photos (seasonal, new team members, new vehicles). - Review your service list. Add any new services you've started offering.

What to ignore

  • Link building for a single-location service business. It's a low-ROI time sink at this scale. Get a link from your chamber of commerce, your local newspaper if you do something newsworthy, and one local sponsor (little league team, charity run). That's enough.
  • Social media beyond GBP Posts. A Facebook page you never update hurts more than helps. Either post weekly or delete it.
  • Multi-location schema. You have one location. You don't need it.
  • AI content generation for service pages. Google's spam policies explicitly target automatically-generated content [8]. Write your own service pages. They're 300 words each. You can do it.

The honest caveat

This plan assumes you can spend one weekend on setup and 1-2 hours per week on maintenance. If you can't, hire it out — a competent local SEO freelancer will charge roughly $500-$1,500/month for a single-location engagement, based on the going rates reported in industry surveys [9]. That's a real range, not a quote.

If you do the work yourself, the 90-day result is predictable: more GBP views, more calls, more reviews, and a ranking position that improves because nothing is broken. Local SEO at the single-location level isn't about clever tactics. It's about not having any broken basics.
