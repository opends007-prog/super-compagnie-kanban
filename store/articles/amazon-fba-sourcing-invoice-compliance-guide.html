Amazon FBA Sourcing Invoice Compliance: How to Keep Your Account Safe

Amazon suspended over 1.2 million seller accounts in 2023 for policy violations and counterfeit concerns. A significant portion of these suspensions trace back to a single operational failure: invalid or missing sourcing invoices. When Amazon triggers a supply chain verification, you have 72 hours to provide documentation proving you bought your inventory from an authorized source. If your invoices fail to meet Amazon's strict formatting requirements, your account goes down.

This guide breaks down exactly what Amazon requires in a sourcing invoice, how to audit your suppliers before a suspension hits, and how to structure your documentation to pass verification on the first attempt.

The Amazon Invoice Verification Standard

Amazon does not accept handwritten receipts, generic order confirmations, or screenshots of online shopping carts. The Amazon Seller Central verification team looks for specific data points that prove a transaction occurred between a legitimate supplier and your registered business.

According to Amazon's official Supply Chain documentation, a valid invoice must contain the following elements:

  • Supplier Information: Name, address, and phone number matching the entity you claim to be buying from.
  • Buyer Information: Your business name and address. This must exactly match the legal entity name and address registered in your Amazon Seller Central account.
  • Invoice Date: The date of purchase must precede the date you listed the products on Amazon.
  • Product Details: A clear description of the items, including quantities. The description must match the product you are selling.
  • Pricing: Per-unit price and total cost.
  • Authorization: The invoice must be signed or authorized by the supplier.

Amazon explicitly rejects proforma invoices and statements of work. They require a finalized, itemized commercial invoice or a formal receipt from a licensed wholesaler.

Source: Amazon Seller Central, "Supply Chain documentation" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200333620)

Common Invoice Rejection Triggers

Most sellers fail invoice verification not because they sell counterfeit goods, but because their paperwork contains easily avoidable errors. Review your existing supplier invoices against this list of common rejection triggers.

Mismatched Business Names

If your Amazon account is registered to "XYZ Holdings LLC" but your supplier addresses the invoice to "John Smith," Amazon will reject it. The business name on the invoice must match your Amazon entity name character for character. This is the most frequent cause of failed verifications.

Missing Authorized Signatures

Amazon requires a signature from the supplier to validate the document. A printed name at the bottom of a digital invoice is not sufficient. The document must feature a physical or verifiable digital signature from an authorized representative of the supplier.

Illegible or Altered Documents

If you scan a paper invoice and the scan is blurry, Amazon will reject it. If you redact pricing to hide your margins, Amazon will reject it. You must provide clear, unaltered, complete documentation. Amazon's verification team handles thousands of these documents daily; they will not spend extra time trying to decipher poor quality scans.

Source: Amazon Seller Central, "Invoice Requirements" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201047710)

Auditing Your Suppliers for Compliance

You cannot wait for an Amazon verification request to find out if your supplier's documentation is inadequate. You need to audit your supply chain documentation proactively.

When you onboard a new supplier, request a sample invoice. Run that sample against the checklist above. If the supplier cannot provide a sample invoice that meets Amazon's criteria before you place an order, they will not be able to provide one during an audit.

The Retail Receipt Trap

Many new Amazon sellers source inventory from big box retailers like Walmart or Target, a practice known as retail arbitrage. Amazon accepts retail receipts, but they must meet the same strict standards as wholesale invoices.

When you buy clearance inventory from a retail store, you must ask the cashier to manually enter your business name and address into the point-of-sale system. A standard receipt showing only the store location, time of purchase, and last four digits of a credit card will not pass Amazon's verification. The receipt must explicitly tie the purchase to your Amazon registered business entity.

Source: Amazon Seller Central, "Sourcing Products" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201744900)

Building Your Compliance Kit

To survive an Amazon supply chain audit, you need a centralized, organized repository of your sourcing documentation. Do not rely on searching through email inboxes when your account faces suspension.

Step 1: Standardize the Collection Process

Require your suppliers to email a finalized PDF invoice immediately upon payment. Do not wait for them to mail physical copies. Create a dedicated email address or cloud storage folder specifically for receiving invoices to ensure they do not get lost in daily communications.

Step 2: Verify the Document Immediately

When an invoice arrives, spend two minutes verifying it against Amazon's requirements. Check the business name match. Confirm the date. Ensure the supplier included a signature. If the document fails any criteria, contact the supplier immediately to request a corrected version. Correcting an invoice is much easier on the day of purchase than it is six months later during an audit.

Step 3: Organize by SKU and Date

Create a filing system that mirrors your Amazon inventory. If Amazon asks for the sourcing documentation for a specific ASIN, you need to locate the corresponding invoice in under five minutes. Use a standard naming convention for your files: [Supplier Name] - [ASIN] - [Date of Purchase].pdf. This allows you to search your drive by ASIN and instantly pull the required paperwork.

Step 4: Maintain a Supplier Contact Log

Amazon sometimes contacts suppliers directly to verify invoice authenticity. If your supplier's contact information is outdated, or if the supplier refuses to confirm the transaction with Amazon, your account remains at risk. Keep a separate spreadsheet with your supplier's primary contact name, phone number, and email address. Update this log quarterly.

Responding to a Verification Request

If Amazon sends you a supply chain verification request, your response speed and accuracy determine the outcome. Amazon gives you a strict deadline—usually 72 hours—to provide the requested documentation.

Do not send a long explanation or a defense of your business practices. Amazon's verification team only wants the documents. Reply to the case with a clear, single PDF or a zip file containing the exact invoices requested. Ensure the file names match the ASINs in question. If an invoice spans multiple pages, combine them into a single PDF. Make it as easy as possible for the reviewer to see that your documentation meets their criteria.

If you sourced the product from a distributor, include the distributor's contact information in your reply. Transparency speeds up the verification process. If you cannot provide a valid invoice for a specific ASIN, remove that ASIN from your catalog immediately. Continuing to sell unverified inventory after a request guarantees a permanent suspension.

Source: Amazon Seller Central, "Submit Invoices" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200519140)

Conclusion

Invoice compliance is not a glamorous part of running an Amazon FBA business, but it is the foundation that keeps your account active. Amazon's verification process is automated and strict; they do not give second chances to sellers who submit incomplete or altered documentation. Audit your suppliers today, standardize your invoice collection process, and build a compliance kit before you receive a verification request. When Amazon asks for your sourcing documentation, you want to be the seller who replies in ten minutes with a perfect PDF, not the seller scrambling to contact a supplier who went out of business six months ago.
