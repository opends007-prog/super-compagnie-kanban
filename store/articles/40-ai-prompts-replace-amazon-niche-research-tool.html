40 AI Prompts That Replace Your $50/Month Amazon Niche Research Tool

Most Amazon FBA sellers burn $30 to $100 a month on niche-research subscriptions like Helium 10's Black Box, Jungle Scout's Opportunity Finder, or Viral Launch. The data those tools surface — search volume, competition density, price bands, review counts — is pulled from public Amazon pages and a few third-party APIs. A well-built prompt against a model with browsing or tool access can produce the same shortlist in one sitting, for the marginal cost of an API call.

This article gives you 40 prompts organized by the actual workflow of finding a niche: idea generation, demand validation, competition mapping, profit math, risk filtering, and final shortlist. Each prompt is paste-ready. Swap the {{placeholders}} for your specifics.

A note on honesty before we start: the prompts below assume you have access to a model that can either browse live Amazon listings or read CSVs you paste in (Helium 10 exports, Jungle Scout exports, or your own scrapes). A vanilla ChatGPT session without browsing will hallucinate numbers. Don't trust any output you can't verify by clicking through to the listing. Treat the AI as a sorting and synthesis layer over real data, not as a data source [1][2].

Phase 1 — Idea Generation (Prompts 1–6)

1. Category sweep

List 20 Amazon subcategories in the {{parent category}} niche that have between 1,000 and 50,000 monthly searches on Amazon, where the average review count on page one is under 500. Flag any subcategory dominated by a single brand.

2. Pain-point mining from reviews

Here are 200 one-star reviews from {{competitor ASIN}}. Cluster them into pain points. For each cluster, suggest a product variant that would solve it. Estimate the addressable monthly search volume by counting how often the pain-point words appear in the reviews.

3. Trend riding

Using Google Trends data for the last 36 months, identify 10 product keywords in {{niche}} with rising search interest but flat Amazon competition (page-one review counts unchanged). For each, give me the trend slope and the current Amazon BSR range.

4. Adjacent niche transfer

I sell {{existing product}}. List 15 product extensions that share my supplier base, my packaging line, or my photography setup. For each, estimate the Amazon search volume and the average price point.

5. Underserved keyword gaps

Take these 50 keywords I already rank for: {{paste keywords}}. For each, find 3 long-tail variants where the top 10 Amazon results have under 200 reviews. Output as a table.

6. Reddit-to-Amazon pipeline

Scan r/Frugal, r/BuyItForLife, r/HomeImprovement, and r/{{your subreddit}} for the last 90 days. Extract every product mentioned positively more than 5 times. For each, check whether the Amazon search results page is dominated by listings with under 300 reviews.

Phase 2 — Demand Validation (Prompts 7–14)

7. Search volume triangulation

For the keyword "{{keyword}}", give me: (a) Helium 10 Cerebro estimated monthly searches, (b) Jungle Scout estimated monthly searches, (c) the count of Amazon reviews on the top 10 ASINs, (d) the average BSR of the top 10 ASINs. If you can't access live data, label each figure as "estimated" and explain your method.

8. Seasonality check

Plot the last 24 months of search interest for "{{keyword}}" using Google Trends. Identify the peak months, the trough months, and the coefficient of variation. Flag any keyword where the trough month has under 30% of the peak month's volume — those are seasonal traps.

9. Click-share vs. conversion-share

For "{{keyword}}", estimate the click-through rate to the top 3 organic results versus the sponsored results. If sponsored results take more than 40% of clicks, organic entry is harder than the search volume suggests.

10. Cross-platform demand

Search YouTube, TikTok, and Pinterest for "{{keyword}}" tutorials or reviews posted in the last 60 days. Count the views. Cross-platform demand above 500K monthly views usually predicts Amazon demand 2–3 months out.

11. Repeat-purchase signal

For {{product type}}, estimate the average replacement cycle. Products with under 6-month replacement cycles build review velocity faster and sustain BSR better than one-time purchases.

12. Gift vs. self-purchase

Classify "{{keyword}}" as gift-purchase, self-purchase, or hybrid based on Amazon search suggestions and autocomplete data. Gift-purchase keywords spike in Q4; self-purchase keywords are steadier.

13. BSR-to-units estimator

Given an Amazon BSR of {{number}} in {{category}}, estimate monthly unit sales. Show your formula. Cross-check against the average review velocity of the top 10 ASINs.

14. Demand decay check

For "{{keyword}}", compare the search volume 12 months ago to today. Anything below 70% of last year's volume is in decay — skip it unless you have a unique angle.

Phase 3 — Competition Mapping (Prompts 15–22)

15. Review-count histogram

For the top 30 ASINs ranking for "{{keyword}}", build a histogram of review counts. If the median is above 1,000, entry is hard. If under 300, entry is open. Anything in between needs a differentiation angle.

16. Brand concentration

For the top 30 ASINs, count how many are owned by the same parent brand. If one brand owns more than 40% of page one, that's a moat.

17. Listing quality audit

Pull the title, bullet points, A+ content, and main image for the top 5 ASINs in "{{keyword}}". Score each on: keyword density, benefit clarity, image quality, and A+ usage. Identify the weakest listing — that's your wedge.

18. Price-band map

For the top 30 ASINs, list the price, the average review count, and the estimated monthly sales. Plot price vs. review count. The empty quadrant (high price, low reviews) is where new entrants can win.

19. FBA vs. FBM split

For the top 30 ASINs, flag FBA vs. FBM. If more than 30% are FBM, Prime buyers are underserved — a FBA listing has an advantage.

20. Review velocity

For the top 10 ASINs, estimate reviews per month over the last 6 months. Anything above 50 reviews/month is hard to catch without a launch budget.

21. Keyword cannibalization

For each of the top 10 ASINs, list the top 5 keywords they rank for. If the same 3 ASINs own the same 5 keywords, that keyword cluster is locked.

22. Image gap analysis

Describe the main product image for each of the top 10 ASINs in "{{keyword}}". Identify the most common visual style. If 8 out of 10 use white-background studio shots, a lifestyle or infographic main image will stand out.

Phase 4 — Profit Math (Prompts 23–30)

23. Landed cost build-up

For {{product}}, calculate the landed cost per unit given: manufacturing cost ${{X}}, MOQ {{Y}}, freight ${{Z}}, Amazon FBA fee for a {{weight}}-ounce, {{size}}-inch item in the {{category}} category. Show the formula.

24. Margin waterfall

Given a sell price of ${{X}}, FBA fee of ${{Y}}, COGS of ${{Z}}, and ad spend of ${{W}} per unit, walk through the margin. Flag any line item above 30% of revenue.

25. Break-even ad spend

For a product with a ${{margin}} contribution margin, how much can I spend on PPC per unit before I break even in month 1? Assume a 25% conversion rate from ad click to sale.

26. Cash flow timeline

Given a ${{X}} inventory buy, a {{Y}}-day manufacturing lead time, a {{Z}}-day Amazon shipping window, and a {{W}}-day payout delay, when does cash turn positive?

27. Review-cost-per-acquisition

If I need 100 reviews in the first 90 days and my conversion rate is {{X}}%, how many units do I need to sell? At a {{Y}}% margin, what's the cost of those reviews in foregone profit if I'm using the Vine program?

28. Storage fee trap

For a {{size}} item, estimate the monthly storage fee at an Amazon fulfillment center. If the product sells slower than {{X}} units/month, long-term storage fees will eat the margin.

29. Return rate estimate

For {{product category}}, what's the typical Amazon return rate? Returns above 15% kill margin because Amazon doesn't refund the return shipping for many categories.

30. Multi-SKU bundle math

If I bundle {{item A}} and {{item B}}, what's the combined COGS, the combined shipping weight, and the realistic bundle price given the price points of the individual items?

Phase 5 — Risk Filtering (Prompts 31–36)

31. Regulatory check

For {{product}}, list every US regulation that applies: FDA registration, CPSC testing, FCC certification, Prop 65 warnings, lithium battery shipping rules. Flag any that require pre-launch testing.

32. IP check

Search the USPTO trademark database for "{{brand name}}" and "{{product keyword}}". List any live trademarks in the same category. A trademark in the same class is a cease-and-desist risk.

33. Hazmat classification

Does {{product}} qualify as Amazon Hazmat? If yes, what's the additional fee per unit and the documentation required?

34. Seasonality risk

If 60% of annual demand for "{{keyword}}" happens in a 90-day window, what's the inventory risk if I order in January for a Q4 peak?

35. Supplier concentration

For {{product}}, list the top 5 suppliers on Alibaba. If the top supplier accounts for more than 50% of listings on Amazon, a supply disruption would clear the niche — but also means the supplier is gatekept.

36. Review manipulation risk

For the top 10 ASINs in "{{keyword}}", check whether their review velocity is consistent with their BSR. Sudden spikes in reviews without matching sales spikes suggest incentivized reviews — risky to compete against because Amazon may suppress the listing.

Phase 6 — Final Shortlist (Prompts 37–40)

37. Weighted scoring

Score these 10 niches on: demand (search volume), competition (review count), margin (estimated profit per unit), and risk (regulatory + IP). Use weights of 30/30/25/15. Output the ranked list with the score for each.

38. Decision matrix

For the top 3 niches from the previous prompt, build a decision matrix with these columns: first-year revenue estimate, break-even month, capital required, time-to-launch, and personal interest score (1–10). Recommend one.

39. Launch plan

For the recommended niche, generate a 90-day launch plan: week-by-week tasks for sourcing, listing creation, photography, PPC setup, and review acquisition. Include specific deliverables for each week.

40. Kill criteria

For the recommended niche, define the metrics that mean I should abandon the product after 60 days: BSR threshold, ad ACoS ceiling, review velocity floor, and return rate ceiling. If any metric is missed, what's the exit plan?

How to actually run this

Pick one model with browsing or tool access. Claude with the computer-use feature, ChatGPT with browsing enabled, or Gemini with the search grounding toggle all work. Paste the prompt, paste your data, read the output, verify every number by clicking through to Amazon.

The prompts above don't replace the data — they replace the clicking, sorting, and deciding. The data still comes from Helium 10, Jungle Scout, or your own scrapes. The thinking still comes from you. The AI just removes the four-hour afternoon [1].
